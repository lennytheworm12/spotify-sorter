## What is defensible in the explanation

The useful hypothesis is that distorted bass or processed vocals can be detected
correctly while their contribution to the song's overall musical identity is
misread. Illangelo describes deliberately contrasting spacious verses with large
choruses, heavy vocal processing and distortion in his account of making The
Hills. Those production devices alone do not establish a hyperpop identity.
[Producer interview in Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inside-track-weeknd).

The supplied Google explanation is too categorical to use as a rule. Fast tempo,
bright affect or ironic delivery should not become necessary conditions for
hyperpop. Waves' production guide discusses lower tempos, trap-derived drums and
serious emotional themes within hyperpop. Its suggested tempo range is also a
production heuristic, not a universal genre definition.
[Waves production guide](https://www.waves.com/how-to-produce-hyperpop-songs-tips).

The claim about the scream's origin also needs qualification: The Weeknd said he
recreated audience noise from an earlier recording.
[Variety interview, syndicated by Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/weeknd-goes-track-track-almost-133030178.html).
This does not independently verify the pilot profile's claim of a female vocal
hook at 42–78 seconds. A scream/effect, an outro voice and a lead hook are distinct
audible claims. The exact retained audio remains the comparison target.

These sources were consulted after all inference and the listening snapshot were
